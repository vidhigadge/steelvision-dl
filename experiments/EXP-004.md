# EXP-004 — Resolution Degradation

## Objective

Measure how reducing image resolution affects steel surface defect classification performance.

The purpose of this experiment is to quantify how much classification accuracy is lost when the visual detail available in the steel surface image is reduced.

This experiment establishes the low-resolution baseline that will later be compared against neural super-resolution methods.

## Model

ResNet-50 transfer-learning model from EXP-002.

The model uses pretrained ImageNet features with a steel-defect classification head.

## Input

All images are ultimately passed to the model at:

**224 × 224 pixels**

However, before being resized back to 224 × 224, each image is first downsampled to a lower effective resolution.

## Method

For each test image:

```text
Original image
      ↓
Downsample to target resolution
      ↓
Upsample back to 224 × 224 using bicubic interpolation
      ↓
ResNet-50 classification
```

The following effective resolutions were tested:

- 224 × 224
- 128 × 128
- 64 × 64
- 32 × 32

Bicubic interpolation is used for both resizing operations.

Resizing a low-resolution image back to 224 × 224 does not restore the lost visual information. It only increases the spatial dimensions of the degraded image so that it can be passed into the neural network.

## Tested Resolutions

| Effective Resolution | Final Model Input |
|---|---|
| 224 × 224 | 224 × 224 |
| 128 × 128 | 224 × 224 |
| 64 × 64 | 224 × 224 |
| 32 × 32 | 224 × 224 |

## Results

| Effective Resolution | Test Accuracy |
|---|---:|
| 224 × 224 | **99.61%** |
| 128 × 128 | **83.92%** |
| 64 × 64 | **49.80%** |
| 32 × 32 | **31.76%** |

## Accuracy Drop

Using the 224 × 224 result as the reference:

| Resolution | Accuracy | Absolute Drop |
|---|---:|---:|
| 224 × 224 | 99.61% | 0.00 percentage points |
| 128 × 128 | 83.92% | 15.69 percentage points |
| 64 × 64 | 49.80% | 49.81 percentage points |
| 32 × 32 | 31.76% | 67.85 percentage points |

## Observations

- The ResNet-50 model performs very strongly when the full 224 × 224 image information is available, achieving **99.61% test accuracy**.
- Reducing the effective resolution to 128 × 128 causes a noticeable decrease in performance to **83.92%**.
- At 64 × 64, classification performance drops sharply to **49.80%**.
- At 32 × 32, accuracy falls further to **31.76%**.
- The experiment shows a strong relationship between available spatial detail and steel defect classification performance.
- The largest deterioration occurs as the images move into the 64 × 64 and 32 × 32 resolution ranges.
- Bicubic upscaling alone is not sufficient to recover the visual information lost during aggressive downsampling.
- The results suggest that low-resolution steel surface images can substantially reduce the reliability of defect classification.

## Interpretation

The network always receives a 224 × 224 tensor, but the amount of real image information contained inside that tensor changes.

For example:

```text
224 × 224 original
        ↓
32 × 32 degraded image
        ↓
224 × 224 bicubic image
```

The final image has dimensions of 224 × 224, but its actual visual information is still derived from only a 32 × 32 representation.

Therefore, increasing the image dimensions after degradation does not recreate the fine surface textures that may be important for identifying defects.

## Research Finding

This experiment provides evidence that classification performance is highly sensitive to image resolution.

The drop from:

**99.61% at 224 × 224**

to:

**31.76% at 32 × 32**

shows that severe resolution degradation removes important visual information required for defect recognition.

This creates the motivation for the next stage of the project.

## Connection to Super-Resolution

The next experiment will investigate whether neural super-resolution can improve low-resolution defect recognition.

The key comparison will be:

```text
Low-resolution image
        ↓
Bicubic upscaling
        ↓
Classification accuracy
```

versus:

```text
Low-resolution image
        ↓
Neural super-resolution
        ↓
Classification accuracy
```

The goal is not only to make the image look visually better.

The main research question is:

> Can neural super-resolution recover useful visual structure well enough to improve downstream steel defect classification?

## Notes

- Bicubic interpolation is used as the non-neural baseline.
- No retraining of the classifier is performed for each resolution.
- The same trained ResNet-50 model is evaluated across all degraded resolutions.
- The experiment isolates image resolution as the main changing variable.
- The original high-resolution test set remains unchanged.
- These results will serve as the reference baseline for Phase 6 super-resolution experiments.
