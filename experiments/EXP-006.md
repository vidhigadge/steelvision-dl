# EXP-006 — Super-Resolution Downstream Evaluation

## Objective

Evaluate whether pretrained Real-ESRGAN super-resolution improves downstream steel surface defect classification compared with standard bicubic upscaling across multiple low-resolution settings.

The main research question is:

> Does neural super-resolution improve defect classification accuracy when the original image resolution is degraded?

## Classifier

Fine-tuned ResNet-50 from EXP-003.

## Super-Resolution Method

Real-ESRGAN x4 using the NCNN/Vulkan implementation.

The NCNN implementation was used because the Python/PyTorch Real-ESRGAN pipeline was too slow for full-dataset evaluation on the available Mac hardware.

## Input Resolutions

The following effective low-resolution inputs were evaluated:

- 128 × 128
- 64 × 64
- 32 × 32

## Evaluation Pipeline

### Bicubic Baseline

```text
Original test image
↓
Downsample to target resolution
↓
Upsample to 224 × 224 using bicubic interpolation
↓
Fine-tuned ResNet-50
↓
Classification
```

### Real-ESRGAN Pipeline

```text
Original test image
↓
Downsample to target resolution
↓
Real-ESRGAN x4 enhancement
↓
Resize enhanced image to 224 × 224
↓
Fine-tuned ResNet-50
↓
Classification
```

## Observations

- Real-ESRGAN did not improve classification accuracy at any of the tested resolutions.
- At 128 × 128, accuracy decreased substantially from **83.92% to 63.53%**.
- At 64 × 64, accuracy decreased from **49.80% to 41.18%**.
- At 32 × 32, performance was nearly unchanged but slightly lower, decreasing from **31.76% to 31.37%**.
- The effect of Real-ESRGAN was strongly class-dependent.
- `scratches` consistently achieved high recall after super-resolution.
- `patches` also retained relatively strong classification performance at moderate degradation.
- `crazing` performed very poorly after Real-ESRGAN enhancement and received zero recall across the tested SR conditions.
- At 32 × 32, `rolled_in_scale` also received zero recall.
- These results indicate that visually sharper images do not necessarily contain more useful discriminative information for the classifier.

## Interpretation

Real-ESRGAN reconstructs plausible high-resolution textures from degraded inputs.

However, these reconstructed textures are not guaranteed to match the true original defect structure.

The fine-tuned ResNet-50 was trained on the original steel-image distribution.

Therefore, when Real-ESRGAN generates new texture patterns, the enhanced images may shift away from the visual patterns the classifier learned during training.

The resulting pipeline can be represented as:

```text
Low-resolution image
↓
Real-ESRGAN reconstructs plausible detail
↓
Image appears sharper
↓
Reconstructed texture may differ from true defect texture
↓
Classifier receives an altered image distribution
↓
Classification performance may decrease
```

## Main Research Finding

The experiment demonstrates that:

> Improved visual resolution does not automatically result in improved downstream defect classification.

In the current SteelVision-DL pipeline, pretrained Real-ESRGAN enhancement reduced classification performance compared with simple bicubic interpolation across all tested resolutions.

This suggests that generic perceptual super-resolution models may not be directly suitable for industrial defect recognition without additional task-specific adaptation.

## Scientific Significance

The purpose of the experiment was not only to generate sharper images.

The real objective was to determine whether reconstructed detail improves the downstream defect-analysis task.

These results show why super-resolution methods should be evaluated using task-specific metrics such as classification accuracy and F1-score rather than only visual quality.

A super-resolution model can generate visually convincing outputs while simultaneously reducing the reliability of a downstream classifier.

## Possible Reasons for the Performance Drop

Possible explanations include:

- Real-ESRGAN was trained for general image restoration rather than steel surface defect reconstruction.
- The model may generate artificial textures that resemble realistic detail but do not correspond to the true defect.
- Fine defect structures may already be lost during severe downsampling and cannot be reliably recovered.
- The super-resolved image distribution differs from the original image distribution used to train the ResNet classifier.
- Different defect classes may respond differently to reconstructed textures.

These explanations should be treated as hypotheses rather than confirmed causes.

## Limitations

- Real-ESRGAN was used as a pretrained generic super-resolution model.
- The model was not fine-tuned specifically on steel surface images.
- The classifier was not retrained using Real-ESRGAN-enhanced images.
- Only classification performance was evaluated in this experiment.
- Visual quality metrics such as PSNR and SSIM were not yet included.
- The results are specific to the current dataset, degradation process, classifier, and super-resolution model.

## Future Work

Possible follow-up experiments include:

- Fine-tuning a super-resolution model on steel surface images.
- Training the classifier using both original and SR-enhanced images.
- Comparing additional SR models such as ESRGAN, SRGAN, or SwinIR.
- Measuring PSNR and SSIM alongside classification accuracy.
- Investigating class-specific effects of super-resolution.
- Evaluating whether task-aware super-resolution performs better than generic perceptual super-resolution.
- Comparing super-resolution performance for detection and segmentation tasks.

## Conclusion

Real-ESRGAN successfully produced higher-resolution outputs from low-resolution steel images, but this did not translate into better downstream classification.

The best-performing method in the current comparison remained standard bicubic upscaling.

This experiment therefore shows that super-resolution should not be assumed to improve industrial defect recognition simply because the enhanced images appear sharper.

The downstream task must be evaluated directly.
