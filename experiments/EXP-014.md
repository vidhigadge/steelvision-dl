# Resolution Degradation Results

Classification accuracy decreased substantially as image resolution was reduced.

Results:

| Resolution | Accuracy |
|---|---:|
| 224 × 224 | 99.61% |
| 128 × 128 | 83.92% |
| 64 × 64 | 49.80% |
| 32 × 32 | 31.76% |

Accuracy decrease relative to the 224 resolution experiment:

| Resolution | Drop |
|---|---:|
| 128 | 15.69 percentage points |
| 64 | 49.81 percentage points |
| 32 | 67.85 percentage points |

This experiment confirmed that substantial discriminative information is lost at lower resolutions.

---

# Super-Resolution Results

Real-ESRGAN was evaluated against bicubic reconstruction.

Results:

| Resolution | Bicubic | Real-ESRGAN | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 128 | 83.92% | 63.53% | -20.39 pp |
| 64 | 49.80% | 41.18% | -8.62 pp |
| 32 | 31.76% | 31.37% | -0.39 pp |

The neural super-resolution pipeline did not improve downstream classification.

The main result is:

```text
visually sharper output
does not necessarily produce
better discriminative features
```

Therefore:

```text
perceptual image quality
≠
classification utility
```

# Final Research Findings

## Finding 1 — Transfer Learning

Partial fine-tuning of ResNet-50 produced excellent performance.

Final accuracy:

```text
99.22%
```

This showed that pretrained features transferred effectively to the steel defect classification task.

---

## Finding 2 — Resolution Matters

Classification accuracy decreased sharply as resolution decreased.

At 32 × 32:

```text
31.76%
```

compared with:

```text
99.61%
```

at the full-resolution experimental condition.

This confirms that low resolution can remove discriminative visual information.

---

## Finding 3 — Super-Resolution Did Not Improve Classification

Real-ESRGAN underperformed bicubic reconstruction at every tested resolution.

Therefore the project does not support the assumption that generic neural super-resolution improves steel defect classification.

---

## Finding 4 — Visual Quality and Model Utility Are Different

A sharper-looking image is not necessarily better for a classifier.

Artificial detail produced by enhancement models can change or distort the features used by the downstream network.

---

## Finding 5 — Detection Can Change Under Resolution Adjustment

YOLO bounding boxes and confidence values changed as the same image was evaluated at different simulated resolutions.

This demonstrates resolution sensitivity in localization models as well as classification models.

---

## Finding 6 — Confidence Is Not Image Quality

Detector confidence sometimes increased after resolution reduction.

Therefore confidence should not be interpreted as an image-quality metric.

---

## Finding 7 — Explainability Adds Useful Context

Grad-CAM and SHAP provide information about model behavior.

They improve interpretation but do not establish physical causality.

---

## Finding 8 — Uncertainty Helps Identify Difficult Predictions

Incorrect predictions had substantially:

```text
lower confidence
```

and:

```text
higher entropy
```

than correct predictions on average.

This supports including uncertainty information in the user interface.

---

## Finding 9 — ONNX Was Effective

FP32 ONNX preserved classifier accuracy while reducing CPU inference latency.

Measured speedup:

```text
1.58×
```

This made ONNX the preferred classification runtime for deployment.

---

## Finding 10 — INT8 Quantization Was Not Worth the Accuracy Loss

INT8 compression reduced model size but caused substantial performance degradation.

The final deployment therefore prioritizes predictive performance over maximum compression.

---

# Final Limitations

SteelVision remains a research prototype.

Important limitations include:

- classification evaluation was performed on a limited dataset
- results may not generalize to all industrial steel environments
- localization performance depends on annotation quality
- the detector validation set was relatively small
- the classifier produces one primary image-level class
- bounding-box localization is not pixel-level segmentation
- coordinate mapping assumes resize-based geometry
- arbitrary cropping is not automatically handled
- rotation is not automatically handled
- perspective distortion is not automatically handled
- Grad-CAM does not prove physical defect causality
- SHAP remains a model explanation rather than physical evidence
- entropy is an uncertainty indicator rather than proof of correctness
- confidence is not a defect severity measurement
- detector confidence is not an image-quality measurement
- resolution simulation does not estimate physical camera distance
- image upscaling does not guarantee restoration of lost information
- Real-ESRGAN may generate visually plausible but non-ground-truth detail
- the trained model weights are not stored directly in GitHub
- production authentication is not implemented
- cloud deployment was outside the scope of the project

---

# Responsible Interpretation

SteelVision can:

```text
classify visible steel surface defect patterns
estimate model uncertainty
highlight classifier-influential image regions
localize visible defect regions
map bounding boxes to uploaded-image coordinates
evaluate controlled resolution changes
```

SteelVision cannot be assumed to:

```text
recover defects that are completely absent from the image
measure physical defect depth
measure engineering severity
estimate true camera distance
guarantee industrial safety
replace expert inspection
```

A scientifically accurate description is:

> SteelVision can evaluate low-resolution imagery, optionally study controlled resolution changes, detect visible defect regions, and map localization coordinates back to the original image space.

# Main Conclusion

The strongest result of the project is not only the high full-resolution classification accuracy.

The experiments also demonstrated that:

```text
resolution loss strongly degrades performance
```

and:

```text
generic neural super-resolution does not automatically recover downstream model performance
```

Real-ESRGAN produced visually enhanced images but reduced classification accuracy compared with bicubic reconstruction.

This highlights an important distinction:

```text
perceptual enhancement
≠
task-specific improvement
```

The final SteelVision system therefore avoids presenting super-resolution as guaranteed recovery.

Instead, the application combines classification, uncertainty, explainability, localization, coordinate mapping, and controlled resolution analysis while preserving the limitations identified throughout the research.
